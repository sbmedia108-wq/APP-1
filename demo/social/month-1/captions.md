## 1. Mon 2026-11-23, 08:00 · LinkedIn · Behind the build: big writing

Image: `posts/2026-11-23-monday-fix-text-size.png`

Most apps are designed by people with good eyesight, holding the phone a foot from their face.

When I started building Ask Mavis I set one rule. If someone can't read it without their glasses, it isn't finished.

So the writing comes in three sizes: Big, Bigger and Biggest. The font is Atkinson Hyperlegible, which the Braille Institute designed for readers with low vision. Every step is read out loud as well.

It isn't a clever feature. It's just the bit most apps skip.

Ask Mavis is a free Android app for people who find tech hard. Coming soon.

## 3. Tue 2026-11-24, 11:00 · Facebook · Scam Watch: fake bank alert

Image: `posts/2026-11-24-scam-watch-bank-alert.png`

Scam Watch: the fake bank alert.

It looks urgent. A big payment you didn't make, and a number to ring "now".

Four things give it away:
1. It's from an ordinary mobile number.
2. A scary amount, to make you panic.
3. "Now". Your real bank gives you time.
4. The number to ring is theirs, not your bank's.

Don't ring the number in the text. Ring 159 instead, which puts you through to your own bank safely (it works for most of the big UK banks), or use the number on the back of your card.

Please share this. Someone you know will get one of these.

## 13. Tue 2026-12-01, 11:00 · Facebook · Scam Watch: the "press 1" call

Image: `posts/2026-12-01-scam-watch-press-1-call.png`

Scam Watch: the "press 1" call.

The phone rings and a recorded voice says an expensive order has been placed on your account. Press 1 to stop it.

Don't press anything. Just hang up.

The warning signs:
1. A recorded voice
2. An order you never made
3. "Press 1"
4. Pressure to do it now

If you're worried, check your account the way you normally would, or ring the company on a number you already trust. Pressing 1 only puts you through to the scammers.

Share this with someone who answers every call. We all know one.

## 14. Wed 2026-12-02, 08:00 · LinkedIn · Behind the build: the helper button

Image: `posts/2026-12-02-feature-call-my-helper.png`

One design decision I keep coming back to: Ask Mavis shouldn't pretend it can fix everything.

Some problems need a person. A router that's actually broken. A bank that wants to see you. A cracked screen.

So when a guide doesn't work, Mavis says "That's okay. It isn't anything you've done wrong." Then it offers a big button to ring a named helper, and one to text them. The text is already written: what you were stuck on, and a polite ask to ring when they can.

The aim was never to replace the family. It's to make the family's job smaller, and the call shorter.

## 21. Tue 2026-12-08, 11:00 · Facebook · Scam Watch: Christmas parcel email

Image: `posts/2026-12-08-scam-watch-christmas-parcel-email.png`

Scam Watch: Christmas parcel "on hold".

Lots of us are waiting on deliveries this month, and scammers know it. This email says a parcel is stuck until you pay a small fee.

Look at who sent it. Not the name, the actual email address. Here it's a made-up web address, not the delivery company's.

The other signs: "Dear Customer" instead of your name, a small fee, and a big button to pay.

Delete it. If you're expecting something, track it on the shop's own website or app.

## 22. Wed 2026-12-09, 08:00 · LinkedIn · Behind the build: why the scam check never says "safe"

Image: `posts/2026-12-09-feature-scam-check.png`

A design choice I want to explain, because it took a while to get right.

The scam check in Ask Mavis never tells you a message is safe.

You tell it what a text or call said, and it looks for the warning signs: a fee for a parcel, a "safe account", a code, gift cards, a rush. If it finds them, it says so plainly and tells you what to do next. If it doesn't, it says it can't see the usual signs, and that this doesn't mean the message is definitely real.

It would be easy to show a big green tick. People would like it. But a tool that sounds sure of itself is exactly what a good scam is counting on.

Better to teach the pause.

## 30. Wed 2026-12-16, 08:00 · LinkedIn · Behind the build: designing with dementia in mind

Image: `posts/2026-12-16-feature-read-aloud.png`

When you design for people living with dementia, one idea keeps coming up: reduce what someone has to hold in their head.

So Ask Mavis shows one big step at a time, never a list. Each step is read out loud. There's a button to hear it again, as often as you like, and a slower reading speed. You can go back a step without starting over. And if something doesn't work, it says that's okay and offers to ring someone.

None of this is clever technology. It's patience, written down.

If you work with people living with dementia, or care for someone who is, I'd really value your view on what we've missed.

## 37. Tue 2026-12-22, 11:00 · Facebook · Scam Watch: gift cards

Image: `posts/2026-12-22-scam-watch-gift-cards.png`

Scam Watch: if anyone asks you to pay with gift cards, it's a scam. Every time.

They might say they're from your bank, the tax office, the council, a delivery firm or even the police. They ask you to buy gift cards and read out the code on the back, often with a story about keeping it secret.

No real organisation works like that. Stop, hang up, and tell someone you trust.

If it's already happened, contact the gift card company and your bank straight away. It's not your fault.
