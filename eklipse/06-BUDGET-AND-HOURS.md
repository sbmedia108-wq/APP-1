# 06. Budget and hours

**Starting point:** Dad DJed in the 90s and has no current kit. You have podcast experience. There are no paying customers yet. Each of you has about 5 hours a week.

**The model:**
- **Eklipse Live:** a booking agency. Dad sells and coordinates. Freelance DJs and acts who bring their own insured, PAT-tested kit do the gigs. Eklipse keeps the difference.
- **Eklipse Studio:** podcasts, delivered by you.
- **The rule:** buy no equipment until the business has paid for it from profit.

> All prices below are estimates for planning. Get real quotes before spending.

---

### Level 2: Recommended (about £500–£900 over the first 3 months)
Shoestring, plus:

| Item | Cost (est.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Solicitor check of the booking contract I draft | £150–£300 | One-off. Protects deposits and cancellations |
| Ads test (only after 3+ reviews) | £100–£150/month for 2 months | Meta ads for engaged couples |

| **Total** | **~£500–£900** | |

### Level 3: Grow (later, paid from profit only)
Only once bookings are steady (for example, 5+ events done and £1,500+ profit banked).

| Item | Cost (est.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Starter DJ controller for Dad to practise on | £150–£300 | Modern DJing is very different from 90s vinyl. Practise before playing paid gigs |
| Second-hand gigging kit (controller, 2 speakers, sub, mic, lights, stands, cables) | £2,000–£4,000 | Lets Dad play gigs himself and keep the full fee |
| PAT testing | ~£50–£100/yr | Required by most venues |
| Paid CRM (HoneyBook, Dubsado) | ~£20–£40/month | When Sheets gets messy (20+ enquiries a month) |

**My recommendation:** start at **Level 1** and move to Level 2 as soon as the first deposit arrives.

## 2. How the money works

**An agency DJ booking (example)**
| | |
|---|---|
| Client pays Eklipse | £850 |
| Freelance DJ fee (they bring kit) | £550–£650 |
| **Eklipse keeps** | **£200–£300** |

- Take a **25–30% deposit** at booking and the balance 14 days before. Pay the freelancer after the event.
- Agree each freelancer's fee before quoting. Never quote a client first and then hunt for a DJ.

**A podcast client (example)**
| | |
|---|---|
| Edit + publish + 3 clips, per episode | £150–£250 |
| 2 episodes a month | £300–£500/month |
| Your time per episode (est.) | 2–3 hours |

*At 5 hours a week, you can serve **one podcast client** alongside your marketing jobs. Price for your time, not just the edit.*

### Year-one scenarios (illustrative)
| | Cautious | Good | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency bookings | 6 | 15 | 25 |
| Profit per booking | £200 | £250 | £275 |
| Live profit | £1,200 | £3,750 | £6,875 |
| Podcast client (months active) | 0 | 6 × £350 | 10 × £450 |
| Studio income | £0 | £2,100 | £4,500 |
| **Gross profit** | **£1,200** | **£5,850** | **£11,375** |
| Minus running costs (Level 1–2) | -£300 | -£800 | -£900 |
| **Left over** | **~£900** | **~£5,050** | **~£10,475** |

On 15 hours a week combined, this is a **side business**, not a salary, in year one. The "Strong" column funds Dad's own kit in year two and makes it a real business.

## 3. Hours: about 5 a week each

### 👨 Dad: the face and the fixer (5 hours)
| Task | Hours/week |
|---|---|
| Answer enquiries, call leads, send quotes (same day) | 2 |
| Build the freelancer roster, then venue outreach and visits | 1.5 |
| Money: deposits, paying freelancers, insurance | 0.5 |
| Weekly check-in with you (Sunday, 30 min) | 0.5 |
| Spare (or DJ practice once he has a controller) | 0.5 |
| **Plus:** attend the first few events in person to check quality | per event |

### 🧑 You: digital and Studio (5 hours)
| Task | Hours/week |
|---|---|
| Social: post 2–3 times a week (content drafted by me) | 1.5 |
| Website, Google profile, listings, replying to reviews | 0.5 |
| Podcast side: pitch clients, then deliver | 2 |
| Bring tasks to me and send what I draft | 0.5 |
| Weekly check-in with Dad | 0.5 |

### 🤖 Me (Claude): marketing and admin team
No hours limit. I work whenever you open a session. Best rhythm:
- **Weekly (about 20 min of your time):** "Here's what happened this week." I write next week's posts, replies, quotes and outreach.
- **Monthly (about 45 min):** Send me the numbers. I review them and adjust the plan.
- **On demand:** quotes, contracts, freelancer messages, website changes, graphics and ads.

### Rules that make 5 hours a week work
1. **WhatsApp Business auto-reply** on Dad's phone: "Thanks! We'll get back to you today with ideas and a price. Or build your quote now: eklipsemedia.co.uk/quote". It covers him while he's busy.
2. **Same-day replies, not instant ones.** Dad checks enquiries twice a day (lunch and evening, 10 minutes each).
3. **Batch the content.** You film and post in one sitting. I write a whole month's captions at once.
4. **One shared Google Sheet** for enquiries, bookings, freelancers and money. Nothing lives only in someone's head.
5. **Sunday check-in, 30 minutes:** what came in, what's booked, what's stuck, and the 3 jobs for this week.
