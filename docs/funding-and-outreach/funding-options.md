Researched 21 November 2026 for a pre-launch app made by a sole trader in Southampton (Android launch around early January 2027).

**How this was checked.** Everything here comes from web searches run on 21 November 2026, and the links are kept. This session couldn't open the funders' own web pages directly because the network blocked them. So "Open?" below shows what the search results said, and **every row needs a quick check on the funder's own site before you apply**. Rows marked ⚠️ rely on third-party listings or undated pages.

## The one thing to know first: legal structure

The business plans set Ask Mavis up as a **sole trader** (`ask-mavis-full-business-plan.pdf`, "Legal, tax and admin"). Most grant funders won't fund a sole trader or a profit-making company. Your routes split three ways:

| Route | What it unlocks | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|
| **Stay a sole trader and partner with a charity** (e.g. an Age UK branch, a carers' centre or a library service). The partner is the applicant, and the project is "digital help sessions for older people using Ask Mavis". | Awards for All, council grants, HIWCF, Comic Relief Community Fund, Lloyds Bank Foundation (all through the partner) | Nothing. The money goes to the partner's project, not to you, but it can pay for sessions, leaflets, tester vouchers and your time as a supplier if the partner agrees. |
| **Set up a CIC limited by guarantee** (asset-locked, not-for-profit) | You can apply to Awards for All yourself ([TNLCF lists "community interest company (CIC) limited by guarantee"](https://tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/programmes/national-lottery-awards-for-all-england)), and a CIC is often eligible for UnLtd, SSE and community-foundation grants | You can't take outside investment for shares. Profits are locked to the community purpose. It needs at least two unrelated directors for most grants. It changes the business plan. |
| **Set up a limited company (by shares)** | SEIS/EIS angel investment and Innovate UK grants | £100 at Companies House and about £500–£1,000 a year for accounts (business plan figures). Most charity grants are closed to it. |

**My suggestion:** stay a sole trader for launch, as the plan says. Win small grants through a partner charity, and apply to UnLtd in January 2027. Only form a company once you know whether you want investors (limited company) or grants (CIC). You can't easily have both.

## Top five, and why

1. **National Lottery Awards for All England.** It's open all year, gives up to £20,000, and "digital help for older people in Southampton" fits it well. Apply now through a partner (e.g. Age UK Southampton runs sessions using Ask Mavis), or yourself once you're a CIC limited by guarantee. Allow 16 weeks.
2. **UnLtd Award (window ⚠️ 6 Jan to 1 Mar 2027).** It's built for early-stage social entrepreneurs, backs people as well as projects, and opens the week you launch. Check now whether a sole trader can apply.
3. **Partner-led local small grants: Southampton Community Chest, Hampshire councillor grants and HIWCF.** These are small sums (£100–£5,000) but realistic. They pay for leaflets, help sessions and tester vouchers, and each one builds a local partner. Note that Southampton city is not covered by Hampshire County Council schemes.
4. **Lloyds fraud prevention scheme and other banks' scam-prevention work.** The scam check (159, 7726) fits closely with what banks are already spending on. It's not confirmed open, so make it one enquiry email and treat it as a partnership lead as much as funding.
5. **SEIS angels (later, as a limited company).** This is the only route to £10,000+ that doesn't depend on a charity partner. It means incorporating and giving up some equity, and the plan shows the app can launch on about £560 without it. Talk to angels after you have 3 months of real install and paying numbers.

Free and useful straight away: join the **Good Things Foundation National Digital Inclusion Network**, ask the **Solent Growth Hub** for free advice, and ask Southampton City Council to add you to its **funding bulletin**.
