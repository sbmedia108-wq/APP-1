## Southampton app maker launches free "Ask Mavis" to help people who get stuck with technology

**A Southampton app maker has built a free Android app that talks people through phone, TV and online problems one big step at a time. It launches on Google Play in [early January 2027].**

Ask Mavis is designed for people who find modern technology hard, starting with older people. The person presses one big button and says what's wrong in their own words, such as "the telly's gone funny" or "I can't send a photo", or types it. Mavis then shows the fix one large step at a time and reads each step out loud.

The app has 156 built-in guides in 13 areas, including the TV and streaming, WhatsApp, email, photos, Wi-Fi, passwords, banking, booking the GP and NHS services, and the ordering screens, self-checkouts and car park machines people meet out and about. The guides work without an internet connection.

There's also a scam check. People tell Mavis what a suspicious message or call said, and it points out the warning signs. It reminds them to ring 159 to reach their bank and to forward scam texts to 7726.

The app uses big writing, with text up to "Biggest", large buttons and strong contrast. It has been tested against accessibility guidance for older users. A "call my helper" button rings a family member. A pretend ordering screen lets people practise at home, and a daily brain teaser with stars and streaks encourages people to keep practising.

Alongside older people, Ask Mavis is being designed for people living with dementia and their carers, people with learning disabilities, and people whose first language isn't English.

The need is real. Age UK says more than 1 in 3 people over 65, about 4.7 million, lack the basic skills to use the internet successfully ([Age UK, 2024](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/latest-press/articles/2024/more-than-1-in-3-over-65s-4.7-million-lack-the-basic-skills-to-use-the-internet-successfully/)).
