### 2 · What Ask Mavis does
planned: 2026-11-26 · status: ready

What is Ask Mavis?

You press one big button and say what's wrong in your own words, like "the telly's gone funny" or "I can't send a photo". You can type it too.

Mavis then shows the fix one big step at a time, and reads every step out loud.

It's made here in Southampton, it's free to download, and it launches on Android in early January.

#DigitalInclusion #OlderPeople #Southampton

### 3 · Why one big button
planned: 2026-12-01 · status: ready

Most help for technology assumes you already know the right words for your problem.

That's why Ask Mavis starts with one big button and no menus. You just say what's happening, in your own words, and it works out what you need.

Then it shows only one step at a time, in big writing, read out loud. You don't get a wall of instructions, and you don't need the jargon.

#Accessibility #DigitalInclusion #UXDesign

### 4 · Two numbers worth knowing
planned: 2026-12-03 · status: ready

Two numbers everyone should know, and worth passing on to parents and grandparents:

📞 **159**: rings your bank safely if a call or message about your account worries you.
📱 **7726**: forward scam texts to it for free.

Ask Mavis has a scam check built in. You tell it what a message or call said, and it points out the warning signs and reminds you of these numbers. The scam check is free and always will be.

#Scams #FraudPrevention #OlderPeople

### 8 · Built for older eyes and hands
planned: 2026-12-17 · status: ready

A few things I've built into Ask Mavis for people who find small screens hard:

• Writing that goes up to "Biggest"
• Large buttons that are easy to tap
• Strong contrast
• Every step read out loud
• A "call my helper" button that rings a family member

It's been tested against accessibility guidance for older users.
