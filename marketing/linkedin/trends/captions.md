## 1. `01-tender-prices-rising.png`

Tender prices are rising again. Is your cost plan?

This month Turner & Townsend raised its UK tender price forecast for the first time in two years, to 3.75% a year for building projects in 2026 and 2027. It points to tariffs, commodity prices and energy costs. BCIS figures show tender prices up 3.2% in the year to Q3 2026, with building costs rising faster still.

The awkward part: new orders are down, so many contractors are absorbing cost rises for now. When workloads pick up, that cushion goes.

If your appraisal still uses last year's inflation figure, three questions to ask:

1️⃣ Which date is your cost plan priced at, and has it been updated?
2️⃣ Does your programme include inflation to the midpoint of construction?
3️⃣ Is your contingency sized for today's market or last year's?

## 2. `02-future-homes-standard.png`

The Future Homes Standard is 4 months away. Is your next scheme costed for it?

From 24 March 2027, new homes in England must meet the new energy rules. The key dates for developers:

📅 24 March 2027: the new rules come into force
📅 24 September 2027: separate timetable for higher-risk buildings
📅 24 March 2028: homes with a building regulations application made before March 2027 must have started on site by this date to stay on the current rules

Miss the start date and those homes have to be built to the new standard. That changes the specification for fabric, heating and ventilation, and with it the build cost and your appraisal.

What we're doing with residential clients now:

✔ Checking which plots can realistically start before March 2028
✔ Costing both routes, current rules and Future Homes Standard, so the decision is based on numbers
✔ Updating cost plans and appraisals before land deals and funding are fixed
